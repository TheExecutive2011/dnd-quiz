The Lantern Fair

A fair by day, a dwarven cistern by night, a monster the town has been feeding for two hundred years, and the thing that drove it out. Everything below is in the order you run it. Orange boxes are read aloud. Numbers in blue are the checks.

Before play · 10 minutes

Getting six people into the game

The three rules to say out loud

  • Tell me what you want to do, in plain words. I will tell you if a roll is needed and which die. Nobody needs to know a rule tonight.
  • When it is your turn in a fight, you get to move and do one thing. Hit something, cast something, help someone, or try something clever. Clever is encouraged.
  • You cannot break the game. Any idea is allowed. Some ideas have consequences. That is the fun.

Introduce the party in character

Go around the table. Each player says their character's name, what they look like, and finishes this sentence: "Hollis Vane hired me because…" You know their class and background from the Sheet; prompt them with it if they freeze. Six sentences, then start.

The truth, for you only

What is actually going on

Brambleford's founders built on top of a dwarven cistern and found an otyugh living in it. Otyughs eat anything and leave the water clean. The town struck a deal: a cart of grain down the well every harvest, and the Keeper keeps the cistern, and so the wells, sweet. Two hundred years later it is a tradition with lanterns, and only the Vane family remembers it was ever a bargain.

A month ago a bulette tunnelled up from the deep rock and broke into the cistern. It drove the Keeper out of its chamber, has been eating the stored grain and anything else it finds, and its thrashing is the tremor people feel at the fair. The Keeper, wounded, stopped eating. The water began to turn. The chains that go down tonight will come up cut, and it will be the bulette that cut them.

Cast

Everyone in Brambleford, with something to say

Each one has a look, a line to open with, and a want. If the party talks to them, use the want. If the party ignores them, nothing breaks.

Hollis Vane

Mayor. Round, sweating, too many rings.

"Security. Just security. Keep the peace until the lanterns, and I will double what we agreed."

Wants: the gift to go down and the night to end like every other year. Knows: the chains came up empty last harvest and the family ledger says that has never happened. He will not admit either until the chains come up cut.

Old Maud Vane

The mayor's aunt. Tiny, sharp, sells lanterns from a handcart.

"You will want one of these. Not for the dark. For what is in it."

Wants: someone to remember what the Keeper is. She is the last Vane who does, and she is a retired priest of the harvest. Later: she is the party's safety net. See Nobody dies.

Tam Hale

Nine. Mud to the knees. Has lost her goat.

"Buttons went down by the old well and he NEVER goes by the old well."

Wants: Buttons back. Will follow the party until told firmly to stop. Later: Buttons is alive in the flooded channel, and bringing him up is the easiest hero moment of the night.

Orrin Hale

Tam's father. The farmer whose cart is the gift this year.

"Best harvest in ten years and the town sends a quarter of it down a hole. Don't tell Hollis I said that."

Wants: to keep his grain. Will offer the party twenty gold to "lose" the cart. If they take it, the gift does not go down and the Keeper scene is harder.

Bram Ironhollow

Dwarf strongman. Barrel-shaped, oiled, loud.

"Lift the bar, win the purse! Nobody lifts the bar!"

Wants: the purse to stay his. The bar is bolted to the stage. Investigation 12 to notice, Athletics 18 to tear it loose anyway. Later: his chains are the best rope in town. He knows the dwarven stairwork under the well is real, because his grandmother told him.

Madame Zel

Fortune teller. Really Zelda Pike, the cooper's wife, in a scarf.

"The cards see… a journey. Downward. Hm. That is new."

Wants: five copper a reading and to be left alone. She is a fraud, but she can feel the tremors before anyone else and it frightens her. Insight 13 to see that part is real.

Goodwife Petra Marrow

Runs the pie table. Flour everywhere. Fierce.

"Taste first. Then judge. Then taste again, to be certain."

Wants: a judge for the pie contest who is not related to any entrant. The party qualifies. Later: the pie table ends up on the gift cart as a bribe for the Keeper, if anyone thinks of it. The Keeper loves it.

Constable Dunstan Reed

Town law. Drunk by noon. Resents the hired help.

"Security. I am security. Thirty years. Never needed strangers."

Wants: to be respected. Persuasion 12 or buying him a drink turns him into an ally who will hold the crowd back at dusk. Insulting him means he arrests someone at the worst moment.

Fennick Pocket

Gnome. Keeps the well winch running. Oily hands, big grin.

"Two hundred years of chain and never a link snapped. Dwarven. You don't get that any more."

Wants: someone to admire the winch. He can lower the party down in the gift cart's cradle, six at a time, and will, if asked nicely.

Isolde Vane

The mayor's daughter. Seventeen. Watching everything.

"My father thinks the Keeper is a story. My great-aunt thinks it is a god. I think it is in the ledger and I think you should read it."

Wants: the truth out. The bone for your experienced players: see Act 1, The ledger.

Wendel Ashby

Brewer. Running the ale tent. Generous after the second barrel.

"First one's for the guards. Second one's for the guards who tell me a story."

Wants: gossip. He knows Dunstan has not been down the well since the "bad year" thirty years ago, when the constable before him went down and came up grey and quit.

The Tolliver twins

Pim and Pol. Ten. Running a goat race with no goats, since one is missing.

"We'll race YOU then. Loser carries the winner."

Wants: to be chased. Athletics 10 to win. They have seen "a big thing with a lot of mouths" through a cellar grate and nobody believes them.

Sister Averil

Young priest of the harvest shrine. Earnest. In over her head.

"The blessing is at dusk. It has always been at dusk. I do not actually know why."

Wants: to do the ceremony right. She has two potions of healing from the shrine's cupboard and will give them to anyone going down the well.

Act 1 · The fair

0:10 to 1:10

Read aloud

Brambleford's green is full. Stalls down both sides, bunting between the chimneys, a stage with a dwarf on it shouting about a bar nobody can lift. The smell is pies and cut hay. Children are everywhere. Lanterns hang unlit from every post, hundreds of them, waiting for dusk. In the middle of the green, roped off and ignored, is an old stone well with a winch over it and a farm cart beside it heaped with grain.

Mayor Hollis Vane finds you before you have finished looking. "Good, good. You're here. Walk around. Be seen. Keep the peace until the lanterns go up. That is all. That is absolutely all."

Then let go of the reins. The party walks the fair. Every stall is a scene from the cast list. Keep these moving; when a scene stalls, a kid runs past, a pie goes missing, or Dunstan starts a fight. Aim to hit the four beats below in any order, with the well last.

Beat · The strongman

talk

Bram's bar is bolted down. A player who notices can expose him or blackmail him. A player who tears it loose anyway becomes the fair's hero and wins the purse: 15 gp. Either way Bram grumbles and offers his chains "for whatever you're really here for."

Beat · The pie contest

talk

Petra needs judges. Three pies, three players tasting. Nothing to roll. Let them argue. The winning pie is Orrin Hale's wife's, which annoys Petra. The pies stay on the table, which matters at dusk.

Beat · The tremor

twist

The ground shivers. Not a shake, a shiver, like a cart going past on the far side of a wall. Pies slide. A lantern post sways. People laugh, the way people laugh when they have decided something is nothing. Across the green, the fortune teller has gone white.

If anyone asks around: it has happened four times this month. If anyone asks Madame Zel: Insight 13 and she admits she feels them coming, "like something walking under the floor."

Beat · The ledger

for the experienced players

Isolde Vane finds whichever player looks like they are paying attention. She has taken the family ledger from her father's study: two hundred years of harvest entries, each one "gift lowered, gift received." Last year's says "gift lowered." Nothing else. And the first page is not a ledger entry. It is a contract, in a hand nobody has written in for centuries, between "the founders of this place" and "the Keeper beneath," granting the Keeper the ground the town stands on, "for so long as the gift is paid."

The experienced players will see it: the town does not own its own land. The Keeper does. It never comes up in the plot unless they bring it up, and if they bring it up at the end, it changes what the town owes the party. That is the whole bone. Let them chew it.

Beat · The goat

talk

Tam Hale, tearful, pulling on sleeves. Buttons went down by the old well. The rope around the well is new. The grass inside the rope is torn up in a line, like something dragged. The stone lip on the far side has a crack in it, and the crack is fresh, and it goes inward. Survival 10: the drag marks go over the lip. Perception 12: something is moving in the shaft, a lot of somethings, squeaking.

Beat · First trouble

fight

The squeaking gets louder. Then the well lip boils over with rats, a river of them, and behind the river come two rats the size of dogs with eyes like wet coal. The fair screams. Everyone is looking at you.

Swarm of rats and 2 giant rats. Map A. This cannot kill anyone and is meant to be won in three rounds. Use it to teach: initiative, "what do you do," attack rolls, one spell. Let a cantrip clear the swarm so a caster gets a moment. When it is over, the crowd cheers. Dunstan arrives late and takes credit.

After: the rats were driven up. Something below made them run. Tam's goat is not among them.

Act 2 · Dusk

1:10 to 1:30

Read aloud

The sun goes behind the mill and Sister Averil rings the shrine bell, and all at once the lanterns are lit, hundreds of them, post by post, until the green is a bowl of warm light. The town goes quiet. Fennick Pocket leans on the winch. Orrin Hale's cart, heaped with grain and, if your players are quick, three prize pies, is hooked to the chains and swung out over the well. Mayor Vane raises a hand. The cart goes down.

You hear the chains paying out, link after link, for a long time. Longer than any well should be. Then they go slack. Then they go tight, so hard the winch jumps. Then they come up fast and loose, and the last link swings into the lantern light. It has been cut. Not snapped. Cut.

The ask

talk

Vane, no longer pretending: the Keeper has kept the water clean for as long as the town has existed. It has stopped eating. The wells have started to smell. He does not know what it is. He does know that in the "bad year" thirty years ago the chains came up slack and the water went bad for a season and a constable went down and would never say what he saw. Go down. Find out what is wrong. Fix it. Pay is double, 300 gp each, and the town's thanks, "which is worth something, whatever Orrin says."

If Isolde's ledger is in play, this is when a sharp player asks what the gift is really for. Vane does not know. Old Maud does.

The lantern

twist

Old Maud Vane pushes through the crowd and puts a lantern into someone's hands. It is older than the others, iron and horn, and it is already lit. "Every gift goes down with one of these. Every year. The Keeper likes the light. It always has." She looks at the cut chain. "Whatever did that does not."

The lantern is just a lantern. What matters is that the party carries it, lit, into the Keeper's chamber. See Act 3.

Ten minutes to be clever

talk
  • Bram's chains and Fennick's winch get them down in the cart cradle, six at a time, 120 feet.
  • Sister Averil gives two potions of healing to whoever asks, and a third if someone is kind to her.
  • Wendel's ale tent has lamp oil, rope, and a story about the grey constable.
  • The pies, if nobody put them on the cart, can still come down in a basket. The Keeper's favourite thing on earth.
  • Dunstan wants to come. He is drunk. Let them decide. He is useless and loud and it is funny, and if he comes he is the one who gets paralysed by the crawlers, not a player.

Act 3 · Under the town

1:30 to 2:40

Read aloud at the bottom of the shaft

The well does not end in water. It ends in a hall. Pillars carved with knotwork go off into the dark in both directions, and the floor is dry flagstone with a channel down the middle where water used to run. The ceiling is low for you and would be right for someone shorter and wider. Waterlines on the walls show where the cistern stood, centuries ago, higher than your head. All along one wall, stacked three deep and going back as far as the lantern reaches, are carts. Farm carts. Hundreds of them. Every one of them empty.

Running the cistern

Keep it opening up

Theatre of the mind until the fights. The rule is that every choice reveals something bigger: a stair down, a flooded channel, a dwarven door into somebody's cellar, a wall of empty carts. Three real destinations: the flooded channel (the goat and the crawlers), the Keeper's chamber (the conversation), and the collapsed grain cellar (the finale). Whatever order they pick, the channel and the Keeper happen before the cellar, because the tremors and the smell lead there last. Two forks are enough. Do not draw a maze.

Checks to sprinkle: History 14 the carvings are dwarven, a cistern for a city that was never built. Perception 13 fresh scrapes on the flagstones, something very heavy dragging itself. Nature 12 the smell is not rot, it is a stomach. Investigation 11 the cut chain links are bitten, not cut, by something with a jaw a yard wide.

The flooded channel

fight

The channel here still holds water, black and waist deep, with a ledge along one side. Something is bleating. On a dry shelf at the far end, a goat with a bell on a string is backed into the corner, and the water between you and it is moving. Three long pale shapes slide up out of it, all tentacles and teeth, and the nearest one lifts its head and looks at you with no eyes at all.

3 carrion crawlers. Map B. The danger is paralysis, not damage. Tell the table what the tentacles do the first time one hits: "You cannot move. You can see and hear everything. Your friends have one minute." Paralysed characters are dragged toward the water, 10 feet a round, and that is the clock. If it is going badly, the third crawler goes for Buttons instead of a player. If Dunstan came, he is the first one paralysed and nobody has to be.

After: Buttons is fine. A player carrying a goat through the rest of the dungeon is worth more than any treasure. On the shelf behind him: a child's lantern, thirty years old, and a constable's badge.

The Keeper

talk or fight

The chamber is round and the ceiling goes up out of sight. Chains hang down the middle of it, cut, swinging slightly. The floor is grain, feet deep, two hundred years of it, and in the grain, backed against the far wall, is the thing the town has been feeding. It is the size of a hay wagon. It has three legs, a mouth you could stand up in, and two long arms that end in more mouths, and on a stalk above all of it, three eyes that are looking at the lantern. Not at you. At the lantern. Something is wrong with its side; it has been torn open and the grain under it is dark. Then a voice that is not a voice, inside your head, slow, like someone waking: "Light. Good. You bring the gift? The gift is late. Something is in my house."

The otyugh. Wounded: it has 60 of its 114 hit points. It speaks into the minds of anyone within 120 feet, in short sentences, like a very old, very large dog that has learned words. It wants three things, in this order: the lantern kept lit, the gift (grain, or better, pie), and its house back.

  • If they keep the lantern lit and talk: it tells them, in pieces, about "the Digger," a thing that came up through the floor a month ago, "all teeth, all shell," that threw it out of its own chamber and ate its stores. It will help kill it if they feed it first. Feed it and it follows them to the cellar. Persuasion 10 with the gift in hand, no roll needed with a pie.
  • If the lantern goes out: it panics. One round of tentacle slams at whatever is closest, then it retreats into the grain. Relight the lantern and it calms. The table will remember this.
  • If they attack: Map C. It fights to the death, because it has nowhere to go. It is a hard fight, but they chose it. If they win, the town's water goes bad for good. That is the ending they bought. Say nothing about it until the wrap.
  • If they leave it: it stays. The finale is fair but hard. It will not come.

In the grain, if they look: Investigation 12. Two centuries of things that fell in. A rusted dwarven helm, 40 gp of mixed old coin, and a sealed clay tube holding a scroll of revivify, offered down the well in the bad year by a priest who hoped it would bring the constable back. It did not; he came back on his own. See Nobody dies.

Act 4 · The Digger

2:40 to 3:15

Read aloud

You smell it before you see it: a sour, hot smell, grain going bad by the ton. The passage opens into a cellar the size of a barn, directly under the fairground; you can hear the fair through the ceiling, faint, a fiddle. Half the cellar has collapsed. Sacks of grain are piled in walls and drifts. Three dwarven pillars still hold the roof up. The fourth is rubble, and beside the rubble is a hole in the floor the width of a cart, going down into black. The floor shivers. The hole breathes dust. Something under the sacks is moving toward you, under them, like a shark under water, and the sacks are sliding off its back.

The fight

fight

1 bulette. Map D. It is a land shark: an armoured hump with a mouth, and it burrows through rock as fast as you walk. It comes up under people. It leaps.

  • Round 1: it erupts from the sacks next to whoever walked in first. Bite. Then it dives back under the grain, and the party learns that the floor is not safe.
  • Sacks are cover. Half cover behind a drift. A player who thinks to set the grain alight makes a wall of fire the bulette will not cross, and a cellar full of smoke in three rounds. Both are allowed.
  • Pillars. Athletics 15 or 20 damage topples one onto the bulette: 3d10 and it is restrained for a round. Each pillar brought down drops rubble from the ceiling, 1d6 to everyone in the cellar. Three down and the fairground floor comes with it.
  • The Keeper's help. If it was fed, it arrives at the start of round 3 through the far passage, grabs the bulette with both tentacles, and holds it. Restrained, no burrowing. The fight turns.
  • It flees at 25 hit points. Down the hole. Unless they stop it. Stopping it is the choice.

The choice at the end

talk
  • Kill it. Chase it down the hole, or finish it before it moves. Its hide is worth 200 gp to Bram, who knows a dwarf who knows a dwarf.
  • Seal it out. Drop the last pillar across the hole. The cellar is lost, the fairground sags a foot overnight, and the deep is closed. The Keeper has its house back.
  • Lure it up. Bram's chains, the winch, a pie. The bulette comes up the well into a fairground full of lanterns and screaming people, and the whole town sees what it has been paying for. Chaos. Nobody dies. Vane never recovers. The experienced players will love this one.

Wrap

3:15 to 3:30

Back on the green. The lanterns are still lit; nobody has had the heart to put them out. Tam has her goat. Vane has his purse out and his hands are shaking. Old Maud is sitting on her handcart, watching you the way she watched the well.

  • Pay: 300 gp each, or more if someone has read the ledger and asks who really owns the ground the mayor is standing on.
  • If the Keeper lives: Maud tells them, quietly, that the wells will be sweet by spring. Tomorrow a new cart goes down, with pie.
  • If the Keeper died: she tells them the same thing, and does not mean it. The town will drink from the river now. Say it gently. It was their choice and it was a fair one.
  • Last line, each player: "It is midnight at the Lantern Fair. What does your character do?" One sentence each. Then stop.

Monsters

The five fights, with numbers

Numbers are from the SRD 5.1 versions, which are the ones most tables know. Your 2025 Monster Manual has newer blocks for all five; if it is open on the table, use those and ignore these. Either works at this level.

How to describe the two nobody has heard of

Otyugh: "Picture a dumpster that grew legs. A bloated, three-legged body the size of a wagon, a mouth down the front of it big enough to walk into, two long rubbery arms that end in smaller mouths, and a stalk on top with three eyes. It lives in filth and eats anything. It is not evil. It is a garbage disposal that learned to think." Show the picture.

Bulette: "A land shark. An armoured hump the size of a cart, plated like a tortoise, with a shark's head and a shark's appetite, and it swims through rock and dirt the way a shark swims through water. You see the ground bulge, and then it comes up under you." Show the picture.

Nobody dies

The safety net, in layers

1 · Monsters stop at zero

Everything down here is hungry, not cruel. A creature at 0 hit points is dropped, not finished: the rats scatter, the crawlers go for the goat or Dunstan, the Keeper backs off, the bulette turns to the next person standing. A downed character makes death saves as normal, which is tense enough, and someone pours a potion into them.

2 · Potions

Sister Averil's two (three) potions of healing before the descent. Two more potions of healing in the Keeper's grain if anyone looks. That is five, for six players, before the boss.

3 · The scroll

A scroll of revivify in the clay tube in the Keeper's chamber. The cleric, paladin, or druid of the group can read it, or any character who holds it out to Old Maud at the end. It brings back anyone who died in the last minute. One use. Place it where they will find it, and if they are not looking, Tam's goat noses it out of the grain.

4 · Old Maud

If someone dies in the finale and nobody has the scroll, the cellar ceiling is right under the fairground. The fight is heard. When it ends, the townsfolk come down with lanterns, Old Maud first, and the retired priest of the harvest kneels by the body with a diamond she has carried for thirty years for exactly this. Revivify, no roll. She does not explain, and she never does it twice.

So: potions keep them up, death saves keep it scary, the scroll undoes one death in the moment, and Maud undoes one more at the end. Nobody leaves the table with a dead character on a first night, and they never have to know how many nets there were.

Treasure total if they take everything: 300 gp each from Vane, 15 gp purse, 40 gp old coin, the dwarven helm (fine, 50 gp to a collector), and 200 gp for the bulette hide. Buttons is not for sale.
