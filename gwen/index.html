Spells

How your magic works. Cantrips are free: cast them as often as you like. Every other spell uses up a spell slot of its level or higher. You have 4 1st level, 3 2nd level, 2 3rd level slots, and you get them all back after a Long Rest. Casting a spell with a higher slot than needed usually makes it stronger.

Spell attack: d20 + 7. Spell save DC: 15. When a spell says "save", the target rolls and needs 15 or more to resist.

Concentration You can only keep one concentration spell going at a time. Starting a second one ends the first. If you take damage, roll a Constitution save (d20 + 3) and get 10 or half the damage, whichever is higher, or the spell ends.

Bonus Action spell rule: if you cast a spell using a slot on your turn, you can't cast a second spell with a slot that same turn. A cantrip is fine.

Rules help

Rolling: checks, saves, attacks

Almost everything is a d20 roll + a number, trying to reach a target the DM knows:

  • Ability check (a skill): d20 + the skill number. Target is a DC (Difficulty Class): 10 easy, 15 medium, 20 hard.
  • Saving throw: d20 + the save number, to resist something.
  • Attack roll: d20 + to-hit number, against the target's Armor Class.

Advantage: roll two d20s and use the higher. Disadvantage: roll two and use the lower. If you have both, they cancel out: roll one.

Natural 20 on an attack always hits and is a critical hit. Natural 1 on an attack always misses.

Your turn: what you can do

On your turn: move (up to your speed, and you can split it up before and after your action), take one Action, and take a Bonus Action only if a spell or feature says it uses one. Talking a little is free.

Actions anyone can take:

  • Attack: swing or shoot a weapon.
  • Magic: cast a spell or use a magic feature.
  • Dash: move your speed again.
  • Disengage: move away without enemies getting free hits.
  • Dodge: attacks against you have disadvantage until your next turn, and you have advantage on Dexterity saves.
  • Help: give a friend advantage on their next check or attack.
  • Hide: Dexterity (Stealth) check to become hidden.
  • Search / Study: look for something (Wisdom) or figure something out (Intelligence).
  • Influence: persuade, deceive or intimidate someone.
  • Ready: pick a trigger ("when the door opens, I cast Fire Bolt") and react when it happens.
  • Utilize: use an object (drink a potion, pull a lever, use a Healer's Kit).

Reaction (one per round, even on others' turns): spells like Shield, or an Opportunity Attack, which means one free melee attack when an enemy next to you moves away.

Hit Points, dropping to 0, and death saves

Damage lowers your HP. Temporary HP absorbs damage first and doesn't stack. At 0 HP you fall unconscious.

At the start of each of your turns at 0 HP, roll a death saving throw: a plain d20, nothing added. 10 or higher = a success, 9 or lower = a failure. 3 successes: you're stable. 3 failures: you die. A natural 20: you wake up with 1 HP. A natural 1: two failures. Taking damage while at 0 = one failure (a critical hit = two).

Any healing at all wakes you up. A friend can also stabilize you with a DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine) check.

Resting

Short Rest (1 hour of downtime): spend Hit Dice to heal. Roll a d8, add your Constitution modifier (+3), and heal that much, as many times as you have Hit Dice left. Some features come back too (it says so on each one).

Long Rest (8 hours, a night's sleep): get back all your HP, all spent Hit Dice, all spell slots and all once-per-day features. Only one Long Rest per day.

Conditions (what they mean)
  • Blinded: you can't see; your attacks have disadvantage, attacks against you have advantage.
  • Charmed: you can't attack the charmer, and it has advantage when it talks to you.
  • Frightened: disadvantage on checks and attacks while you can see the source; you can't move closer to it.
  • Grappled: speed 0; you can try to escape with an Athletics or Acrobatics check as your action.
  • Incapacitated: you can't take actions, bonus actions or reactions; concentration breaks.
  • Invisible: attacks against you have disadvantage, yours have advantage.
  • Paralyzed: Incapacitated, can't move or speak; hits from within 5 feet are critical hits.
  • Poisoned: disadvantage on attack rolls and ability checks.
  • Prone: lying down. Standing up costs half your speed. Your attacks have disadvantage; melee attacks against you have advantage, ranged ones disadvantage.
  • Restrained: speed 0; your attacks and Dexterity saves have disadvantage; attacks against you have advantage.
  • Unconscious: Incapacitated, Prone, drop what you're holding; hits from within 5 feet are critical hits.
Cover, range, light

Half cover (behind a low wall or another creature): +2 AC and Dex saves. Three-quarters cover (arrow slit, thick tree): +5. Total cover: can't be targeted.

Ranged attacks at long range (the second number, like 80/320) have disadvantage. So do ranged attacks while an enemy is right next to you.

Dim light: disadvantage on Perception checks that rely on sight. Darkness: effectively blind, unless you have Darkvision, which treats darkness as dim light.
